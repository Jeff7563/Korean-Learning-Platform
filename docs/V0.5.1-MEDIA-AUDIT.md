## Resource access decisions

- **Thai MOOC K-MOOC001:** exact course title is *ภาษาเกาหลีเบื้องต้น 1 세계로 통하는 한국어 : 초급 I | A bridge to the world: Korean Language for Beginners I*. Its public page reports closed registration and contains course description, a six-week/twelve-lecture outline, and schedule topics. We found no unauthenticated course lesson playback on that page. We retain it only as a labeled syllabus reference, not a lesson/video.
- **Online King Sejong Institute:** its public curriculum says Beginner Korean supports Thai and covers practical everyday communication. Its website exposes login/register controls and course pages; course lesson playback/enrollment state may vary. We link the curriculum overview only and do not call it a playable lesson.
- **HRD Korea:** the Thai standard textbook PDF is directly accessible and contains Thai explanations and practice. It targets EPS-TOPIK learners, so it is supplemental for this general beginner course. The separate 2024 revised resource page lists Korean textbooks and listening files; it is a catalog, not lesson playback. HRD Korea states KOGL Type 3 (attribution, no derivatives) for those listed materials. The app links to sources and does not copy their content.
- **Thai PBS ALTV:** the Korean Pop Up episode pages provide public video program content and identify host Jongbae Park. Two of the three linked episodes cover multiple syllabus objectives; each summary in the player states its actual scope.
- **YouTube and OpenHangul:** public access and metadata were checked for the linked KHEM videos. All ten OpenHangul URLs returned HTTP 200; titles matched the Thai lesson topics. Provider, network, regional, or browser changes may affect future availability.

No suitable verified Thai teaching video was found for every Everyday Korean objective. The unserved topics are not assigned unrelated media. Their authored Thai explanations, Korean examples, vocabulary, listening/repetition prompts, writing activities, and checkpoints remain the primary lesson material.

## Learning content QA notes

The Everyday Korean lesson text now distinguishes conversational polite `해요체` from the more formal `합니다체`; separates asking nationality (`어느 나라 사람이에요?`) from asking place of origin (`어디에서 왔어요?`); and spells out Sino-Korean 0–20 construction, including telephone-zero `공` versus general `영`. Existing checkpoint question prompts, options, answer indexes, and explanations were not changed. The new integrity test checks that each of the ten lessons retains complete teaching and checkpoint structures; it does not replace review by a qualified Korean-language teacher.

## Data and compatibility

This work changes authored source content, media metadata, presentation labels, tests, and documentation. It does not run migrations or seed the learner database. Lesson IDs/slugs and stored question keys are unchanged; no checkpoint history, learner answers, progress, or planner data were written. Playwright is configured with a unique temporary database as documented in `playwright.config.ts` and `docs/DATABASE-SAFETY.md`.
